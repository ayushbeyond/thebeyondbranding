Brand & founder playbook · for MAATIQA Studio · November 2026

Every piece is a map of itself.

Seen from the side, a MAATIQA vessel is a stack of layer lines. Seen from above, the same layers become contour lines, like a topographic map of the earth it's made from. That one fact gives the brand a visual language no other ceramics studio owns, and it's honest: it's literally how the pieces are made.

What this playbook settles
  1. A look that can't be mistaken for other 3D-print studios
  2. The mint palette, done so it feels premium
  3. Wordmark vs M monogram: both, with clear jobs
  4. Ten founder videos, scripted
  5. A growth plan, and what we can take on next
01 · The idea

The layer line from the side. The contour from above.

The current identity already uses the layer line. Adding the top view, the contour, makes it a complete system: horizontal stripes for the making, organic contours for the earth. Your team already likes the contour pattern; it becomes the brand's signature surface.

From the side: layers

Every surface is made of stacked coils. Used as horizontal hairline stripes on packaging, the website and reel covers.

From above: contours

The print path seen from the top: nested, twisting rings. Each form gets its own contour drawing, made from its real profile.

Together: the brand

Mint contour field as the ground, the real terracotta piece as the hero. The landscape is the brand; the object is the product.

02 · Standing apart

Right now MAATIQA looks like the category, not the studio.

At 110 pixels (the size of a profile picture in search) a dark-olive circle with a light line mark reads as "3D-printed ceramics studio", not as MAATIQA. CeraPrint already sits there, with 34.8K followers. Moving to a light kaolin-mint ground with a forest wordmark separates MAATIQA immediately, and it's the direction the founder was already leaning.

03 · Palette: Kaolin & Contour

Mint, yes, but dusty, mineral mint, never candy.

The founder's instinct for a lighter mint is right. Done as a soft kaolin green (the colour of a clay slip or old patina, and of the contour pin she liked), it sits opposite terracotta on the colour wheel, so every real clay piece glows on it. Forest stays, but as the ink rather than the background.

Mint is the ground

Backgrounds, packaging, story covers, the profile picture. Keep it flat; the contour pattern is the only texture.

Forest is the line

Wordmark, type, contour lines, icons. Never a big dark field again, except one night-mode band for "behind the material" stories.

Clay is the product

Let the real terracotta carry the warmth. Use the clay colour only for one call-to-action or a launch tile, and kiln ochre only for workshop and firing moments.

04 · Wordmark and monogram

Keep the wordmark for recall. Give the M a job of its own.

This isn't either/or. A new brand needs its name remembered first, so the stacked "MAa / tiQa" wordmark stays as the profile picture and primary logo; it's distinctive and legible at small sizes. The founder's M ideas become the maker's mark, pressed into the clay of every piece, sealing every box, and used as the favicon. Once people recognise the brand, the M can take over the profile picture later.

Primary · keep

Stacked wordmark

Profile picture, website, signage, invoices. Its swash Q is already the most characterful letter in the category.

Direction A · sketch

Layered M

The M on the studio wall, as nested extrusion lines. Closest to what the team already has.

Direction B · sketch

The maker's seal

A round seal for stamping clay, wax and boxes. Thick enough to survive being pressed into wet clay.

Direction C · sketch

The coil M

One continuous line, the way a nozzle draws, starting from a clay-coloured dot. Animates beautifully.

06 · Why the founder should be on camera

People trust the maker before they trust the logo.

The founder appearing on camera isn't vanity; at MAATIQA's stage it's the cheapest trust-builder available. The studio's own reels already show it: the ones with a person and a story beat the product-only reels.

26% vs 19%

Consumers who trust a founder's personal post compared with a corporate post, in Clutch's June 2026 survey of 408 consumers.2

No. 1

The founder's on-camera character

The maker who explains the machine.

  • Curious, not salesyShe shows what she's figuring out: why a print collapsed, why the clay mix changed. Viewers come back to learn.
  • Hands first, face secondNot every video needs a face. Hands, voice and the work is enough, and easier to keep up.
  • Two voicesWhen both founders appear, give each a lane (design and machine, clay and finish) so it never feels like a group photo.
  • Her own account tooCollab posts from her personal profile plus the studio account double the reach of every founder video at no cost.
07 · Ten founder videos, scripted

Say it in your own words. Keep the structure.

Each script has the first-3-second hook, the beats and a caption. Hinglish is welcome; reading lines word for word isn't. Film everything vertical, 25–45 seconds, with captions burned in.

08 · Growing the brand

Beyond Instagram: where the bigger orders come from.

Instagram builds the audience; hotels, architects and press bring the larger, repeatable orders. A small effort on each, every month.

Filming setup

One corner, always ready

  • A permanent mint backdrop or wall near a window
  • Phone on a tripod at nozzle height, plus a clip-on mic
  • Batch-film on print days: one long print gives three reels
  • Same opening frame style every time, so the feed reads as a series
LinkedIn

For architects and hotels

  • The founder posts every two weeks: one finished commission, one material insight
  • Connect with Indore and Mumbai interior designers and hospitality buyers
  • Send a short PDF lookbook on request, not a price list
Press & features

Be the story editors want

  • Pitch Indian design and culture titles that already feature ceramic studios, such as LBB, Homegrown and Beautiful Homes4
  • The angle: "a printer that coils clay like a potter", plus the Layer Passport
  • Keep a press kit ready: 10 photos, founder bio, one-paragraph story
Places

Pieces in real spaces

  • Lend 3–5 pieces to an Indore café or boutique hotel, with a small card and QR code
  • A pop-up or exhibition every season
  • Workshops as the offline community engine
Gifting

The calendar sells for you

  • Diwali, weddings and corporate gifting: small-batch sets with the green seal
  • Take bulk orders by enquiry; they suit the made-to-order flow
  • Start building a gifting list 8 weeks before each festival
Proof

Collect it from day one

  • Ask every buyer for one photo of the piece at home
  • Repost with credit; add the best to the website
  • Write each commission up as a short case study

Reel system

Cover templates, caption style, editing of founder videos each month, plus a filming checklist for print days.
