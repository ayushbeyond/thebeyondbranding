Todoist data breach: what to do.

Who is affected, what was taken, and five steps to secure your account.

By AyushUpdated 27 Nov 2026

Todoist, the popular to-do list app, confirmed a security breach on 24 November 2026. Here is what happened in plain language and what you should do.

Quick answerIf Todoist didn't email you, the company says your account wasn't part of the breach and you don't need to do anything. If it did, reset your password, change it anywhere else you reused it, and reconnect or replace any linked apps and API tokens.

What happened

An attacker used a stolen developer credential to get into Todoist's systems, with activity on 22 November and access to production systems on 23 November 2026. Todoist began emailing affected users on 24 November and published a security notice.

What was taken

WhoWhat was exposed
About 6% of users (mostly long-time accounts)Email addresses, account creation dates and old salted SHA1 password hashes
A tiny fraction of accountsAPI tokens, Slack integration tokens, and some Outlook add-in data

The old hashes belonged to accounts whose passwords hadn't changed since Todoist stopped using that method more than a decade ago. Todoist says it found no sign the attacker signed in to those accounts.

What to do if Todoist emailed you

  1. Reset your Todoist password. Affected passwords were disabled, so you'll be asked to set a new one. Open the app or site directly instead of tapping links in emails.
  2. Change the same password everywhere else. If you used your Todoist password on any other site, change it there too. Old hashes can be cracked over time.
  3. Replace API tokens. If you use a Todoist API token in scripts or automations, generate a new one.
  4. Reconnect integrations. Slack and the Outlook add-in may have been disconnected; Todoist switched the Outlook add-in off on 24 November.
  5. Clean up secrets in tasks. If you ever stored passwords, keys or recovery phrases in task comments, change them and move them to a password manager.

Watch for phishing: scammers often send fake "security alert" emails after a breach. Check the sender carefully and never enter your password from an email link.

FAQ

Was my Todoist account hacked?

Todoist says only accounts that received its email were affected. If you got no email, it says you don't need to do anything.

Were my Todoist tasks stolen?

For most affected users, the data was email addresses, sign-up dates and old password hashes. A very small number of accounts also had tokens and some Outlook data exposed.

Should I delete Todoist?

That's your call. If you stay, reset your password, turn on two-factor authentication and avoid storing secrets in tasks.
